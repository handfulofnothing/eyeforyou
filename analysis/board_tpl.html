How events snap

  • Section cuts → bar 1 of their loopThe source restarts its music exactly where each section's transition begins, so these cuts already fall on a downbeat. They move 2–66 ms.
  • Hard cuts inside a transition → next downbeatIn the source, the three mid-transition hard cuts fall on beat 4. They move to the next bar line, and the beats after them follow in order.
  • Hits → nearest beatPops, hovers, clicks and wipe landings. A click that triggers a cut takes the 8th-note pickup, so the cut still lands on the "1".
  • Ripples → 8ths from a beatDot chains, word builds and nav pops step in 8th notes, starting on a beat.
  • Moves → time-scaledContinuous motion starts on its hit and is stretched or squeezed to land on the next one.

How this feeds the build

  • One page, one clock. A 1920×1080 HTML file. window.seek(t) computes every style from the timings above, with no CSS animations, timers or carried state. It awaits image decodes before resolving.
  • Grid in code. Beat time = block anchor + n × 60/BPMblock. The storyboard's IDs become the keys in the timeline table.
  • 3D safety. The zoom-through and ring moves use preserve-3d only on inner layers. Fades and blurs go on a flat wrapper. Match-cut targets (hub ring, dots, logo eye) are measured at runtime.
  • Render. Playwright shoots 3 subframes per frame (t − 1/240, t, t + 1/240), then tmix blends them to 60 fps: 5,092 frames from 15,276 shots.
  • Probe first. One frame per scene (24 total) goes up for review before the full render, checked for clutter, overlap and legibility.
